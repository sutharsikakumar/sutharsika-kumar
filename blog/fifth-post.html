Tunnel Vision and Self-Doubt

9 July 2026

A Life Well-Lived?

As my birthday approaches, I naturally start to reflect on the life I have lived so far. This has been an ongoing endeavor ever since I graduated high-school. Twenty is seemingly a big number. In fact, I never even contemplated it. But what I can confidently say is that I've faced some of my biggest fears this year. I'm also quite thankful for the placement of my birthday, almost at the midpoint of the year, as it gives me time to reflect on the good, the bad, and the things I want to change.

Here are the bullet points. Retroactively speaking, they seem quite nominal, nothing remarkable. However, each represents a core belief that I had to break down in order to move forward. As easy as it is to talk about these things, they are infinitely more challenging to internalize and practice (so I realized).

Things I'm proud of:

  • Getting over my fear of rejection, at least a little bit.
  • Sharing how I feel about people, things, and events.
  • Taking a class I never thought I was capable of taking, namely Real Analysis.
  • Writing more consistently, not being afraid to share my experiences, and not fearing judgment.
  • Pursuing technical challenges without being deterred.

Thank you to the people who believed in me when I didn't believe in myself, especially my Analysis professor, my friends, and my family. I never thought I was capable of doing math at the level required for upper-level math classes. What if I was a fraud?

The good things are always easy to share. Now comes the hard bit, the bit that would actually change how I approach the rest of the year. A little bit of background is that things may not always work out the way you think they will, and adapting to those changes without complaint is something that is hard to do but greatly respectable. Continue chipping away at it. This is tunnel vision.

Improvement:

  • Something that has come up over and over again in conversations with people is the idea of not having a village without being villagers. You can't have meaningful connections without sacrificing convenience; that seems to be the general consensus. Our lives are so busy, hustling and locking in, but I hope to take more time to spend with the people I love.
  • Taking the easy way out. Sometimes I optimize for the grade, with valid reason, of course. Our grades are a direct representation of how we are doing in classes, but I want to spend time picking apart the true functioning of certain concepts in mathematics and computer science so that I can find the problems, understand the solutions, and apply them to something completely different.
  • Spending more time outdoors. There is so much beauty to be experienced in this world.
  • Pursuing creative expression: art, dance, writing, and, last but not least, baking.

I have also started to notice that there is a balance between talking to people and doing the important work. The foundation is key. There must be material upon which you build things, and I have started noticing that the more interested I am in something, the more tunnel vision I get. To see it through. So I will be seeing these things through, reforming, and pushing myself. And most importantly, don't let a "no" stop you from doing something. Note to self.

Live every day experiencing new things, be open to new ideas and perspectives, and take it all in. We only have this life.

I hope so.
