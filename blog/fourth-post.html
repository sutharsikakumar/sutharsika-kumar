My Love Letter to Ducktales (2017)

1 July 2026

My love for this show knows no bounds. But here it is: DuckTales (2017) might be the greatest animated children's show ever made, with Avatar: The Last Airbender (ATLA) being a close second, more on that later (maybe).

The way I see it, at its core, DuckTales is a story about family. Not family in the greeting-card sense, but family as a representation of inheritance, loyalty, resentment, ambition. The strange obligations we carry toward the people who know us best. The show moves constantly between the long history of Clan McDuck and the adventures of the triplets and Webby, but underneath all of it sits the same question. That is, what does it mean to belong to a family without disappearing inside of it? Why do we carry such responsibility? What makes the show exceptional is that it refuses to separate meaning from humor. It touches politics, capitalism, nationalism, history, and power, but always through absurdity. Ma Beagle delivers a rousing speech about Beakley—smart, cunning, British, a killing machine—only to declare that these are the Beagle Boys, the American flag waving proudly behind her. Mark Beaks invents BeaksCoin. Scrooge's enemies devote their lives to defeating him and end up trapped inside the very ambitions that drive them. The jokes never dilute the ideas; they reinforce them.

I've spent a lot of time trying to figure out why I love this show as much as I do (I've watched it over five times from start to finish), and I keep coming back to the idea that it blends adventure, sarcasm, and sincerity in a way almost no story manages. Every character seems to carry a different human impulse, pride, curiosity, envy, loyalty, insecurity, obsession, the need to be seen. Then the show places these people side by side and asks a quietly radical question, what happens when people with entirely different fears and desires are forced to love each other anyway? The villains are no exception. Magica De Spell. Glomgold (formerly known as Duke Baloney). Lunaris and the Moonlanders. F.O.W.L. The Beagle Boys. The list is endless. Each of them have a compelling backstory that characterizes their actions in a way that I find a lot of stories miss.

The best villains are rarely evil in a simple way. They live in the space between injury and cruelty, juggling humiliation and revenge. What makes the villains of DuckTales so compelling is that their worst qualities are distortions of perfectly ordinary desires. Glomgold wants recognition. Magica wants power. Beaks wants relevance. Lunaris wants superiority. None of these wants are foreign to anyone. The tragedy is simply what they become when left unchecked. That may be the show's greatest strength, it understands that heroism and villainy are not always opposites. Often they begin in exactly the same place. Ambition, grief, love, the desire to matter, and diverge only in what a person does with the wound.

And beneath all the treasure maps and curses, the show keeps circling harder truths. That family can be deeply isolating even as it holds you. That personal responsibility doesn't end where love begins. That luck plays a real role in anyone's success, but that determination and consistency are what carry you in the long run. It's a cartoon about ducks. It also might be one of the most honest things ever written about what we owe the people we love.
